## Purpose
This runbook was created following INC-2026-005, in which an untracked certificate expiry caused an 80-minute outage on the CrestPay API gateway. It covers both routine scheduled rotation and emergency rotation of expired or compromised certificates.

## Detection
All externally facing certificates are now monitored with automated alerts at 30, 14, and 3 days before expiry, and again on the day of expiry if renewal has not completed. Certificate private keys for supported services are HSM-backed per ARCH-004.

## Steps
1. On the 30-day alert, open a renewal ticket assigned to the service owner listed in services.json.
2. Generate a certificate signing request; for HSM-backed keys this is generated within the HSM boundary per ARCH-004 and does not require key export.
3. Submit the CSR to the approved certificate authority and obtain the signed certificate.
4. Deploy the new certificate to a staging environment first and verify handshake success.
5. Deploy to production during a low-traffic window where possible; for the CrestPay gateway, reload rather than restart the service to avoid dropping in-flight connections.
6. Verify certificate validity and expiry date on the live endpoint after deployment.

## Escalation
If a certificate has already expired, treat this as an emergency case: declare at minimum SEV2 per POL-003 and follow RB-001 in parallel for customer-facing gateway impact. Engage Amali Fernando if there is any indication the expiry was caused by a process failure affecting other certificates bank-wide, as occurred after INC-2026-005.

## Rollback
If a newly deployed certificate causes unexpected handshake failures, for example due to an incomplete certificate chain, revert to the previous certificate only if it has not yet expired; otherwise proceed forward with troubleshooting the new certificate rather than reverting to an expired one. Note also that NorthGate performs its own certificate rotations on their side of the integration from time to time, most recently discussed at MTG-2026-011; these should not trigger false alerts in our monitoring.
